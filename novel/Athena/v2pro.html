Prologue

In the dimly lit room, five desks faced each other.

Illuminated by spotlights, the five desks were occupied by women who held the titles of the heads of the First through Fifth Academies.

They were engaged in a regular meeting of the academy heads, which typically proceeded with calm reports and discussions. However, today was different.

Kase Natsuko, the head of the Third Academy, which was the Mermaid School, leaned forward with her elbows on the table, hands clasped in front of her mouth, watching the increasingly heated meeting.

She had her platinum blonde hair elegantly braided and tied at the back, and her slightly tanned skin gave her a warm appearance. Dressed in a chic gown, she exuded a calm demeanor.

All the participants in this meeting wore similar dresses, as there was a shared understanding among them that they should act as role models for the Valkyries.

Natsuko covered her mouth with her hand, suppressing a smile.

(How adorable that she’s getting worked up over a jab at me.)

The cause of her amusement was Himawari Lunaar, the head of the Fifth Academy. Unlike Natsuko, she wore a flamboyant pink gothic dress that revealed her neckline, giving her an air of a nurturing young lady. However, today she was passionately delivering her thoughts.

“Are you familiar with the Prometheus Project?”

With that unexpected topic, she took the floor.

“I believe the birth of a humanoid weapon that can be piloted by men, created through inhumane experiments, is a dangerous plan that jeopardizes our organization and our positions. Ideally, we should freeze the project early or take more forceful measures… but someone is hiding it.”

While maintaining her smile, Himawari's gaze pierced Natsuko with a sharp intensity.

Natsuko felt exasperated by the accusatory look that seemed to label her a traitor.

The others in the room were also viewing her as a betrayer.

All of them were former Valkyries from the first generation, veterans who had survived fierce battles.

Though they now appeared calm, they retained the spirit of the Valkyries from their active days.

In this meeting, the chairperson was the head of the First Academy, which primarily protects the American continent.

The chairperson addressed Natsuko.

“Kase Natsuko, it seems I must demand a detailed explanation from you. Even if we were comrades who fought in the same sky, I will show no mercy to a traitor.”

Her tone was that of a calm lady, but the content was nothing short of a threat.

The surrounding gazes turned harshly toward Natsuko.

Natsuko stopped observing and placed her hands on the table. Ignoring Himawari's glare, she fixed her gaze on the head of the First Academy.

“It’s all for the sake of defeating them—the Gijuu.”

When she mentioned victory, the head of the First Academy nodded, urging her to continue.

Taking into account their current situation, Natsuko explained the usefulness of the Prometheus Project.

“I was initially skeptical of this project. I thought it would be a bonus if it could at least serve as part of the emotional education for the students. After all, they spend their adolescence in a girls' school, which inevitably distances them from common sense.”

Her initial goal was to provide social learning so that the Valkyries raised in the sheltered environment of the academy could survive in the outside world.

The other academy heads listened to Natsuko's explanation with wry smiles or bitter expressions, likely sharing similar concerns.

The chairperson smiled awkwardly.

“We share the same worries, but I think your approach may be too stimulating for the younger generation. If they become attached, it could lead to a decrease in combat effectiveness.”

“Yes, I understand. However, I trust both the students and him.”

“You really haven’t changed at all.”

The atmosphere of the meeting lightened slightly, but Himawari alone looked dissatisfied.

Natsuko was unaware that only the Fifth Academy was addressing the issue of the students' lack of common sense.

Though frustrated, she recognized it as a problem that needed solving.

To tighten the relaxed atmosphere, Natsuko adopted a serious expression.

“However… he has produced results.”

He, referring to Hifumi Ren, the test pilot of the prototype experimental machine known as Thunderbolt.

Having volunteered for illegal experiments, he was originally a seasoned infantryman.

Unlike the Valkyries, who fought in the skies, infantrymen battled in the mud on the ground, and the organization known as the Fairy Agency, which dealt with Gijuu, derogatorily referred to them as ants.

Now, Hifumi Ren, a former infantryman, was assigned as a student at the Third Academy, one of the Valkyries' learning institutions, as the only male student among all the girls.

Initially, Natsuko did not believe that the Prometheus Project, aimed at turning men into combatants against Gijuu, would succeed.

However, for better or worse, Hifumi Ren had accomplished it.

“He achieved victory against numerous second-class Gijuu all by himself. I believe this is a result that cannot be ignored.”

At the mention of “cannot be ignored,” Himawari, seated close to Natsuko, shot up from her chair with vigor.

Seemingly unable to contain herself, she appealed to the chairperson.

“Yes, it cannot be ignored. It’s clear that the old faction will cling to this fleeting hope and try to drag us down. Chairperson, I propose we immediately halt the Prometheus Project.”

While other academy heads seemed inclined to agree with Himawari, Natsuko raised her voice to draw attention.

“No, I advocate for the continuation of the Prometheus Project.”

“Natsuko!”

Himawari shot a fierce expression at Natsuko, furrowing her brows.

However, Natsuko pressed on, addressing the other academy heads.

“Even if the situation is stable, you all know that since the battle with them began, humanity's right to exist has been continuously shrinking, right?”

Himawari and the other academy heads fell silent, their expressions turning bitter as they considered Natsuko's words.

Though humanity had gained the ability to counter Gijuu with the emergence of the Valkyries, they had not made progress in reclaiming lost territory.

There were many reasons for this, one of which was the issue of combat power.

“We have reached our limit in securing further combat power. Yet, in the face of their overwhelming numbers, we are not only failing to reclaim lost territory but are being gradually pushed back. If that’s the case, the Prometheus Project could become a beacon of hope for us, right?”

The militarization of men was something Natsuko welcomed.

However, the reactions of the academy heads present varied.

Himawari, despite recognizing the problem, harbored strong resistance.

“In that case, we should focus all our efforts on the Valkyrie enhancement plan. Unlike us first-generation Valkyries, the current third generation can maintain a stable number. Once the fourth generation is born, even stronger Valkyries should emerge.”

Listening to Himawari, Natsuko feigned ignorance and teased her.

“I heard that the development of that fourth generation is facing difficulties. Am I mistaken? We shouldn’t judge based on hopeful assumptions.”

“—You!”

Teased, Himawari glared at Natsuko with an even more severe expression, looking as if she might leap at her.

Seeing this, the chairperson intervened.

“The behavior of the two of you is quite unladylike. —Your true selves are showing.”

Himawari snapped back to reality at the chairperson's words, slightly blushing as she cleared her throat exaggeratedly.

“I-I apologize.”

Natsuko straightened her posture and also apologized to the chairperson.

“I will be more careful.”

The chairperson sighed softly at their behavior before making a judgment on the Prometheus Project.

“Natsuko has a point. I believe it is premature to halt the Prometheus Project.”

At those words, Himawari glared at the chairperson, but the chairperson remained unfazed.

“—However, simply winning against second-class Gijuu does not qualify as combat power. Unless they can defeat at least a first-class Gijuu, I believe it will be difficult to continue the project.”

Gijuu are classified into ranks based on their strength and threat level, with first-class Gijuu being the ones the Valkyries frequently engage.

Second-class Gijuu can be defeated even by Valkyrie candidates.

To be recognized as combat power, they must achieve victory over first-class Gijuu.

Natsuko shrugged slightly.

“I feel that the current hurdle is too high for them.”

In response to Natsuko's request for relaxed conditions, the chairperson who decided to continue the project gave her a cold look.

“Personally, I also stand against the Prometheus Project. To add, it’s not just Himawari who wants to push for its cancellation.”

When Natsuko glanced at Himawari, she wore a shadowy smile.

“There are no allies for you here. Although everyone present has different positions, none of them have any expectations for the Prometheus Project. In fact, they harbor a strong desire to crush it.”

Though Natsuko found herself isolated in the meeting, she had anticipated this development and remained calm.

In fact, she considered it fortunate that she had not been forcibly told to halt the project.

“Since the project will continue, I can’t ask for more luxuries. I was actually worried it might be canceled.”

Unable to bear seeing Natsuko's frustrated expression, Himawari coldly retorted.

“Do your best on your own.”

The surrounding gazes were skeptical or even hostile toward Natsuko.

She felt a twinge of loneliness from the looks her former comrades directed at her.

(I never imagined I would come to miss those days I wanted to forget.)

The chairperson, concluding that the discussion had ended, announced,

“This regular meeting is now adjourned.”

With that, the other academy heads began to disappear one by one.

As the dim room was illuminated, it revealed Natsuko's office at the Third Academy.

“How impressive that factional disputes arise in this situation.”

Though there were training institutions for Valkyries from the First to Fifth Academies, they lacked cohesion.

“There’s no way the project will continue without any issues. There will definitely be interference. It would be helpful if at least our academy could come together—”

Moving closer to the window, Natsuko gazed out at the academy grounds.

“Our students are strong-willed, so they won’t easily come together. Now, what should I do?”

Natsuko sighed, resting her hand on her cheek.
